How to use this simulation

  1. Choose the kind of circle with the tabs at the top (↔ horizontal · ↕ vertical · ↗ at an angle · ⚙️ gears), then a situation in the bar under them. The situations: 🪑 rotating chair (view from above or from the side) · 🛰️ satellite · 🚗 car on a bend · 🎠 playground carousel · ⚙️ gears: two meshing gears, a gear train with an idler and two gears on one shaft, a bicycle chain, or a motor, belt and pulley lifting a load · 🎢 loop-the-loop · ✂ string breaks (a ball whirled in a horizontal circle, gravity ignored; press ✂ Cut the string on the scene and it flies off along the tangent; the FBD shows F_c = T, then the v arrow after the cut; view from above or from the side) · ⛓️ conical pendulum (swing ride) · 🚗 banked road.
  2. Set each value with ▼ and ▲ (hold to go faster; the ↑ ↓ keys or the mouse wheel in the box also work, Shift = ×10), or type a value in the box. ↺ puts it back to its start value. Outside the classroom range the box turns amber ⚠: fine to explore, but the lab can't do this.
  3. Move the pointer over any graph (or tap it): a dashed line shows the values where it cuts each curve, or the values of the nearest data point.
  4. Press ▶ Start. The scene is a top view: the blue arrow is the velocity v (along the tangent), the orange arrow is the acceleration a (towards the centre).
  5. The middle column is the free-body diagram seen from the side, with only real forces (weight, normal force, friction, tension or grip, gravity). The dashed orange arrow is their resultant: this resultant is the centripetal force mv²/r. There is never an extra "centripetal force" arrow.
  6. 🚗 Car: the forces are labelled with symbols only (mg, FN, Ff), and the dashed orange Fc (the net force) starts on the car and points to the centre. Under the diagram: the equation for Fc and the friction rules — static Ff ≤ μsFN (grips), dynamic Ff = μdFN (sliding) — with the coefficients of the chosen road. On a banked road, tick Components (top of the FBD) to split FN and Ff into horizontal and vertical parts (thin dashed arrows). The horizontal parts are drawn head-to-tail, so they end exactly where ΣF ends: Fc = FN sin θ ± Ff cos θ. Vertically: FN cos θ balances W ± Ff sin θ.
  7. 🎢 Loop-the-loop (a vertical circle; slow motion): energy is conserved, so the object is slowest at the top and fastest at the bottom. The FBD shows the forces now and, under it, the forces at the top · side · bottom to the same scale (orange frame = where it is now). The dashed orange Fc is the inward part of the forces (= mv²/r). If vtop < √(gr), FN reaches zero before the top: the cart leaves the track and falls as a projectile. ⛓️ Conical pendulum: set the period; the chain angle follows from cos θ = g/(ω²L). Here the tension is written FT, because T is the period.
  8. ◂ / ▸ (or the ← → keys) step the motion and pause it. Graph tools: Tangent gives the gradient at that moment (gradient of x–t = vx, of vx–t = ax); Area shades the area under a velocity or acceleration graph up to that moment.
  9. ● Record saves a trial. The Data tab plots your trials and draws a best-fit line; its gradient is a physical quantity (written next to the line).
  10. Work through the Tasks tab. Write explanations on your worksheet.

The physics

  • T = 1/f · ω = 2π/T · v = 2πr/T = ωr
  • a = v²/r = ω²r (towards the centre) · F = mv²/r = mω²r
  • Satellite: gravity provides the force: GMm/r² = mv²/r ⇒ v = √(GM/r); the mass of the satellite cancels.
  • Car on a flat bend: static friction provides the force: μsmg ≥ mv²/r ⇒ vmax = √(μsgr); the mass cancels.
  • Vertical circle: top: F_N + mg = mv²/r · side: F_N = mv²/r · bottom: F_N − mg = mv²/r (T instead of FN for a string); energy: v_bottom² = v_top² + 4gr; slowest safe speed at the top v = √(gr); always F_bottom − F_top = 6mg.
  • Conical pendulum: F_T cos θ = mg · F_T sin θ = mω²r · r = L sin θ ⇒ F_T = mω²L, cos θ = g/(ω²L).
  • Gears (kinematics only): meshing gears and a chain or belt pass on the same rim speed v = ω₁r₁ = ω₂r₂, so ω₂/ω₁ = r₁/r₂ = N₁/N₂ (teeth are all the same size, so r ∝ N). Meshing gears turn in opposite directions, a chain or belt keeps the direction; an idler gear only flips the direction. Gears fixed on one shaft have the same ω but different v. rpm = revolutions per minute; ω = rpm × 2π/60.
  • Rotating chair, beyond SL free spin: nothing turns the chair, so Iω stays the same (angular momentum, HL topic A.4). Pull the arms in → smaller I → larger ω.

Model: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m. Chair: body + chair I = 2.0 kg m² (arms included), hands at 0.20 m when closed. Car: sliding friction μd = 0.8 μs. Carousel radius 2.0 m. No air resistance. Gears: module 4 mm (pitch radius = N × 2 mm); bicycle chain pitch 12.7 mm, wheel radius 0.34 m.

Newton's laws

The badge above the scene says which law applies at this moment: 1st law — at rest or moving at a constant velocity, the resultant force is zero (ΣF = 0); 2nd law — accelerating, ΣF = ma. The table under the free-body diagram adds up the forces (ΣF) and compares them with m a (▾ folds it). Tick 3rd-law pairs to see every force with its partner: the same size and type, in the opposite direction, acting on the other body (the Earth, the floor, the string, the fluid…).
