How to use this simulation

  1. Choose an object, the launch speed and angle. Press ▶ Launch.
  2. Set each value with ▼ and ▲ (hold to go faster; the ↑ ↓ keys or the mouse wheel in the box also work, Shift = ×10), or type a value in the box. ↺ puts it back to its start value. Outside the classroom range the box turns amber ⚠: fine to explore, but the lab can't do this.
  3. Move the pointer over any graph (or tap it): a dashed line shows the values where it cuts each curve, or the values of the nearest data point.
  4. The solid path is the real one, with air resistance; the dashed path is the same launch in a vacuum.
  5. The free-body diagram shows the weight W, the drag Fd (always opposite to the velocity) and their resultant.
  6. Press ● Record. The Data tab plots the range against the angle, with and without air.
  7. ◂ 0.1 s / 0.1 s ▸ (or the ← → keys) step the motion back and forward and pause it, so you can discuss each moment: the velocity and acceleration arrows stay on the screen. Graph tools: Area shades the area under a velocity graph (= displacement) or an acceleration graph (= change in velocity) up to that moment and writes what the area is (its name and value) inside it; Tangent draws the tangent there (gradient of a position graph = velocity, of a velocity graph = acceleration).
  8. Work through the Tasks tab. Write explanations on your worksheet.

The physics

  • Drag: Fd = ½ρCdAv², opposite to the velocity. It grows quickly with speed.
  • Light, large objects (ping-pong ball, shuttlecock) are affected most: their drag soon becomes comparable to their weight.
  • Effects: shorter range, lower top, steeper fall than rise, time down longer than time up, best angle below 45°.
  • Falling fast enough, drag balances the weight: mg = ½ρCdAvt² (terminal speed).

Model: ρ = 1.20 kg m⁻³, Cd constant for each object, no wind and no spin. At SL, air resistance is treated qualitatively; the numbers here help you see the effects.

Newton's laws

The badge above the scene says which law applies at this moment: 1st law — at rest or moving at a constant velocity, the resultant force is zero (ΣF = 0); 2nd law — accelerating, ΣF = ma. The table under the free-body diagram adds up the forces (ΣF) and compares them with m a (▾ folds it). Tick 3rd-law pairs to see every force with its partner: the same size and type, in the opposite direction, acting on the other body (the Earth, the floor, the string, the fluid…).
