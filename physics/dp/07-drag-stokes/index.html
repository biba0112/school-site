How to use this simulation

  1. Ball in a liquid: choose the liquid, the ball and its radius, then press ▶ Drop. The stopwatch times the ball between the two bands A and B (20 cm apart), like Station 5. Press ● Record after each drop.
  2. Set each value with ▼ and ▲ (hold to go faster; the ↑ ↓ keys or the mouse wheel in the box also work, Shift = ×10), or type a value in the box. ↺ puts it back to its start value. Outside the classroom range the box turns amber ⚠: fine to explore, but the lab can't do this.
  3. Move the pointer over any graph (or tap it): a dashed line shows the values where it cuts each curve, or the values of the nearest data point.
  4. Slow runs take a while — use ×5 or ×20. The times shown are always real times.
  5. The Data & analysis tab plots the terminal speed against r².
  6. Skydiver: press ▶ Jump, then ☂ Open parachute whenever you like.

The physics

  • Stokes' law (small sphere, slow smooth flow): Fd = 6πηrv.
  • Buoyancy: Fb = ρfluidVg with V = 4/3 πr³. Weight W = ρballVg.
  • Terminal speed when W = Fb + Fd: vt = 2r²(ρball − ρfluid)g / (9η), so vt ∝ r².
  • Stokes' law only works when the Reynolds number Re = ρfluidv(2r)/η is small (Re < 1). Fast balls in water and skydivers are far outside it: there the drag grows roughly with v².

Model: the drag on the ball is 6πηrv × (1 + 0.15 Re^0.687) (a standard correction that is ≈ 1 when Re is small). Skydiver: drag = ½ρCdAv² with ρ = 1.2 kg m⁻³; area × Cd = 0.70 m² body, 37.5 m² with the parachute — qualitative only.

Newton's laws

The badge above the scene says which law applies at this moment: 1st law — at rest or moving at a constant velocity, the resultant force is zero (ΣF = 0); 2nd law — accelerating, ΣF = ma. The table under the free-body diagram adds up the forces (ΣF) and compares them with m a (▾ folds it). Tick 3rd-law pairs to see every force with its partner: the same size and type, in the opposite direction, acting on the other body (the Earth, the floor, the string, the fluid…).
