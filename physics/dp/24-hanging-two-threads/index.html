How to use this simulation

  1. Choose an example at the top: 🧪 lab (a string between two clamp stands) · 🐦 bird on a wire · 🚶 tightrope walker · 🏮 lantern · 🚦 traffic light · 🪧 sign · 🧺 washing line. The Example list below has more set-ups for each one.
  2. Set the mass m and the angles θL and θR of the threads below the horizontal with ▼ ▲ (hold to go faster; ↑ ↓ keys or the mouse wheel in the box, Shift = ×10) or type a value; ↺ puts it back. Tick same on both sides for a symmetric set-up. Outside the classroom range the box turns amber ⚠.
  3. Choose the thread: cotton breaks above 20 N, fishing line 100 N, nylon cord 2 kN, steel wire 20 kN.
  4. The mass is held by a hand. Press ▶ Let go. If both threads are strong enough, it hangs at rest. If not, the thread with the larger tension snaps: the mass swings on the other thread, which may snap too. ◂ / ▸ (or ← →) step through it.
  5. The middle column shows the free-body diagram of the mass, the vector triangle and a table of the components.
  6. Move the pointer over a graph (or tap it) to read values. ● Record saves a trial when the mass hangs at rest; the Data tab plots T against 1/sin θ (gradient mg/2) and the horizontal and vertical components.
  7. Work through the Tasks tab. Write explanations on your worksheet.

The physics

  • The mass is at rest, so the resultant force on it is zero: ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0.
  • Horizontal: TL cos θL = TR cos θR
  • Vertical: TL sin θL + TR sin θR = mg
  • Same angle on both sides: 2T sin θ = mg, so T = mg / (2 sin θ).
  • Only the vertical components hold the mass up. A flat thread (small θ) needs a huge tension, so a loaded thread or wire can never be perfectly straight. With different angles, the steeper thread has the larger tension.
  • The three forces drawn tip to tail make a closed vector triangle.

Model: g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹; light threads that do not stretch; supports 4.0 m apart at the same height. After a snap the mass swings on the other thread (with a little air resistance) or falls freely. The drawings of the objects are not to scale.

Newton's laws

The badge above the scene says which law applies at this moment: 1st law — at rest or moving at a constant velocity, the resultant force is zero (ΣF = 0); 2nd law — accelerating, ΣF = ma. The table under the free-body diagram adds up the forces (ΣF) and compares them with m a (▾ folds it). Tick 3rd-law pairs to see every force with its partner: the same size and type, in the opposite direction, acting on the other body (the Earth, the floor, the string, the fluid…).
