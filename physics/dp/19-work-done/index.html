How to use this simulation

  1. Choose an example at the top: 📐 a box pulled at an angle · ⛰️ lifting a load or pushing it up a ramp · 🛒 the Smart Cart pulled with a rubber band (class demo) · 🚗 a car braking.
  2. Pick a preset (class demo, slide examples, exit questions) or set the values yourself. Set a value with ▼ ▲ or type it in the box.
  3. Set each value with ▼ and ▲ (hold to go faster; the ↑ ↓ keys or the mouse wheel in the box also work, Shift = ×10), or type a value in the box. ↺ puts it back to its start value. Outside the classroom range the box turns amber ⚠: fine to explore, but the lab can't do this.
  4. Move the pointer over any graph (or tap it): a dashed line shows the values where it cuts each curve, or the values of the nearest data point.
  5. Press ▶ Start. ◂ / ▸ (or the ← → keys) step through the motion and pause.
  6. The middle column shows the free-body diagram (to scale) and the work done by each force (or a Sankey diagram of where the energy goes). Forces at 90° to the motion do no work; friction does negative work.
  7. Graphs: the Area tool under an F–s graph gives the work done; under v–t it gives the distance. The Tangent tool on Ek–s gives the resultant force.
  8. ● Record saves a trial; the Data tab plots it (best-fit line through the origin).
  9. Work through the Tasks tab. Write explanations on your worksheet.

The physics

  • W = Fs cos θ  (1 J = 1 N m = 1 kg m² s⁻²)
  • θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. θ = 90°: no work (normal force, weight on a level floor). θ = 180°: negative work (friction, brakes).
  • Variable force: work = area under the F–s graph (spring: W = ½kx²).
  • Work–energy principle: Wnet = ΔEk = ½mv² − ½mu². Braking: Fbs = ½mu², so the braking distance ∝ u²: twice the speed, four times the distance.
  • Lifting at constant speed: work done = gain in gravitational potential energy mgΔh, whatever the path. The ramp needs a smaller force over a longer distance (and more work if there is friction).

Model: g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹; friction Ff = μFN (the same μ for starting and sliding); the lift and ramp move at a constant slow speed (so ΔEk = 0); the Smart Cart pull is fitted to the PASCO lab 07 sample data (0.500 kg, 0.475 J over 0.60 m, rolling friction 0.03 N).

Newton's laws

The badge above the scene says which law applies at this moment: 1st law — at rest or moving at a constant velocity, the resultant force is zero (ΣF = 0); 2nd law — accelerating, ΣF = ma. The table under the free-body diagram adds up the forces (ΣF) and compares them with m a (▾ folds it). Tick 3rd-law pairs to see every force with its partner: the same size and type, in the opposite direction, acting on the other body (the Earth, the floor, the string, the fluid…).
